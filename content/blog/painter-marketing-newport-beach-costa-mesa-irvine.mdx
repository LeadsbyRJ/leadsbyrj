When a homeowner in Newport Beach decides the house needs a fresh coat, or a property manager in Irvine needs units repainted between tenants, the first stop is almost always Google. They search "house painters near me," "exterior painting Costa Mesa" or "cabinet painting Irvine," look at a few companies, and request two or three estimates.

If your painting business isn't in that short list, you never get the chance to bid. And painting is a trade where the estimate is where most jobs are won.

This guide walks through the steps that help painting contractors in Newport Beach, Costa Mesa, Irvine and nearby cities like Huntington Beach, Tustin and Laguna Beach show up in those searches and turn more of them into booked estimates.

## Why Painting Leads Work Differently

A few things make painting different from emergency trades like plumbing or HVAC:

- **Customers shop around.** Painting is rarely an emergency. Most homeowners compare several painters before they pick one, so your website and reviews do a lot of the selling before you ever show up.
- **Looks matter more than anywhere else.** People are hiring you for how the finished job will look. If your own website and photos look rough, that is the first impression they get of your work.
- **The service mix is wide.** Interior repaints, exterior repaints, cabinet refinishing, stucco and trim work, HOA and commercial jobs. Each one is a different search with a different buyer.
- **Local conditions shape the work.** Homes near the coast in Newport Beach, Corona del Mar and Huntington Beach deal with salt air and sun. Many Irvine and Tustin neighborhoods have HOA color rules. Talking about those things shows you actually know the area.

Good painter marketing speaks to all of that.

## Step 1: Get Your Google Business Profile Right

For "near me" searches, the map results are often the first thing a homeowner sees. Your Google Business Profile is what puts you there.

### Pick the right categories

Set your primary category to "Painter" or "Painting contractor," whichever fits your business best. Add secondary categories only for work you actually do. Adding categories for services you don't offer can bring in the wrong calls.

### List every service separately

Add each service with a short, plain description: interior painting, exterior painting, cabinet painting, trim and door painting, stucco painting, drywall repair before paint, and commercial or HOA painting if you do it. These help Google match you to more specific searches.

### Set an honest service area

List the cities you really want to work in, such as Newport Beach, Costa Mesa, Irvine, Huntington Beach, Tustin and Laguna Beach. Don't stretch it across the whole county if you won't drive there.

### Post photos every week

Painting is visual, and your profile should show it. Upload before-and-after photos from real jobs, with the homeowner's permission. Shots of crews prepping, masking and protecting floors also help, because they show care. A steady stream of fresh photos gives people a reason to click on you instead of the next listing.

## Step 2: Build a Website That Sells the Finish

Your website is where a homeowner decides whether you are worth an estimate. For painters, it needs to do three things well.

### Lead with your best work

Put strong project photos near the top of the home page. Before-and-after pairs work especially well. Skip stock photos of smiling models holding rollers. Homeowners can tell, and it makes your real work look less believable.

### Make the estimate request easy

Put your phone number and a "Get a Free Estimate" button at the top of every page. Keep the form short: name, phone, city, type of project and a notes box. Let people upload a photo if they want. Every extra field you add loses a few leads.

### Make it fast and mobile friendly

Most people will look at your site on their phone, often while standing in the room they want painted. If the site is slow or hard to use on a small screen, they hit the back button and call someone else.

## Step 3: Create a Page for Each Service

One page that says "we do all kinds of painting" won't rank well for any specific search. Give your main services their own pages:

- Interior house painting
- Exterior house painting
- Cabinet painting and refinishing
- Stucco and trim painting
- Commercial, HOA or property management painting

On each page, explain what the job involves, how you prep, what kind of paint or finish you typically use, how long it usually takes, and what the homeowner should expect while you are there. Add photos from that type of job. Answer the questions people actually ask you on estimates, like whether they need to move furniture or how long cabinets take to cure.

## Step 4: Add Real Local City Pages

If you want to rank in Newport Beach, Costa Mesa and Irvine, it helps to have a page for each city you serve. The key is to make each one genuinely useful, not the same text with the city name swapped.

### What to include on a city page

- The neighborhoods you work in, such as Corona del Mar, Balboa Island or Newport Coast for Newport Beach, or Woodbridge, Northwood and Turtle Rock for Irvine
- Photos of jobs you have done in that city
- Local considerations, like coastal exposure near the beach or HOA color approval in planned communities
- A clear way to request an estimate

### Talk about HOA rules where they apply

Many Orange County communities require approval before exterior color changes. If you help homeowners with color submittals or know how the process usually works, say so on the page. It is a real concern for buyers and a good reason to choose you.

## Step 5: Get More Reviews and Use Them

Reviews are one of the biggest things homeowners look at when they compare painters.

### Ask at the right moment

The best time to ask is during the final walkthrough, when the customer is happy with how the house looks. Send a text with a direct link to your Google review page that same day. Make it one tap.

### Ask for specific details

Customers can mention the type of job and the city in their own words, for example "exterior paint job in Costa Mesa." Don't write reviews for them, but you can let them know that details are helpful for other homeowners.

### Reply to every review

Thank people by name and mention the project. If you get a negative review, respond calmly and offer to make it right. Future customers read those replies too.

## Step 6: Show You Are Licensed, Insured and Careful

Hiring a painter means letting a crew into your home for days. People want reassurance.

- Show your California contractor license number on your site and profile if you have one, and make sure it is accurate.
- Mention that you are insured.
- Explain your prep and cleanup process: covering floors and furniture, masking, protecting landscaping on exterior jobs, and cleaning up each day.
- If your crew is your own team rather than subcontractors, say so.

These details answer the worries people have before they even ask.

## Step 7: Turn Estimates Into Booked Jobs

Getting the estimate request is only half the job. Painting customers often collect multiple bids, so how you follow up matters.

### Respond fast

Call or text back as quickly as you can. The first painter to respond and set an appointment often has the advantage.

### Send clear, written estimates

Spell out what is included: surfaces, number of coats, prep work, paint line or finish, and timeline. A clear estimate is easier to say yes to than a single number on a business card.

### Follow up once or twice

If you don't hear back, a polite follow-up a few days later is normal and expected. Many jobs are won simply because one painter checked in and the others didn't.

## Step 8: Track What Is Working

You don't need fancy software to start. Ask every new customer how they found you and write it down. Over a few months, you will see whether leads are coming from Google Maps, your website, referrals or somewhere else. Then you can put more effort into what is actually bringing in work.

## Common Mistakes Painters Make Online

- Using stock photos instead of real project photos
- A website with no clear phone number or estimate button
- One generic services page instead of separate service pages
- No mention of the cities and neighborhoods they serve
- Letting the Google Business Profile go months without new photos or posts
- Never asking happy customers for reviews

Fixing even a few of these can make a noticeable difference in how many estimate requests you get.

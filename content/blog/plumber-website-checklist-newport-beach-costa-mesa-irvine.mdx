When a pipe bursts in a Corona del Mar condo or a water heater dies in an Irvine townhome, nobody scrolls through ten pages of results. They grab their phone, search "plumber near me" or "emergency plumber Costa Mesa," and call one of the first few businesses that look trustworthy.

If your website doesn't show up there, or it shows up but looks dated, loads slowly, or hides your phone number, that call goes to someone else.

The good news is that most plumbing websites in Orange County lose calls for the same handful of fixable reasons. This checklist walks through what your site needs to rank locally and turn visitors into booked jobs, in the order I'd tackle it.

## Why Your Website Still Matters if You Have a Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile gets you into the map results. Your website is what Google uses to confirm what you do, where you do it, and whether you're a real, established business. It's also where a lot of homeowners go to decide between you and the plumber listed right below you.

A weak site holds your map rankings back and makes your profile work harder than it should. A strong site supports your profile, ranks on its own in the regular results, and closes the deal when someone is comparing options.

## 1. Make Calling You the Easiest Thing on the Page

Plumbing is an urgent-need service. Most visitors aren't browsing. They want to know if you can come out and how to reach you.

### What to check

- **Click-to-call phone number at the top of every page.** On mobile, it should be one tap to dial. Test it yourself on your own phone.
- **A sticky call button on mobile** that stays visible as people scroll.
- **Your hours and whether you offer emergency service**, stated plainly near the top. If you do 24/7 calls, say so. If you don't, say when you're available.
- **A short quote or booking form** for non-urgent jobs like fixture installs or remodel work, with only the fields you actually need.

If someone has to hunt for your number, you've already lost a share of those visitors.

## 2. Build a Page for Each Core Service

One "Services" page with a bulleted list isn't enough. Google ranks pages, not whole websites, so each service you want to be found for needs its own page.

### Pages most OC plumbers should have

- Emergency plumbing
- Drain cleaning and clogged drains
- Water heater repair and replacement
- Tankless water heater installation
- Leak detection and slab leak repair
- Sewer line inspection and repair
- Repiping
- Fixture and faucet installation

### What goes on each service page

- A clear headline that says the service and area, like "Water Heater Repair in Newport Beach and Costa Mesa"
- A plain-English explanation of the problem and how you fix it
- Signs a homeowner might notice that mean they need this service
- What the visit looks like, from the call to the finished job
- Real photos of your work, not stock images
- A call button and form on the page

Write the way you'd explain it to a customer standing in their garage. That's also what tends to rank, because it answers the questions people actually type into Google.

## 3. Create Real City Pages, Not Copy-and-Paste Ones

If you serve Newport Beach, Costa Mesa, Irvine, Huntington Beach, Tustin and Laguna Beach, it helps to have a page for each of your main cities. But Google has gotten good at spotting thin pages where only the city name changes.

### How to make a city page worth ranking

- **Mention the kinds of homes you actually work on there.** Older homes in Costa Mesa's Eastside, beach cottages and newer builds on the Newport Peninsula, HOA-managed communities across Irvine, hillside homes in Laguna Beach. Different housing means different plumbing problems, and you know them better than anyone.
- **Talk about the local issues you see.** Hard water buildup on fixtures and water heaters is a common complaint across Orange County. Coastal air can be tough on exposed metal. Older neighborhoods often have aging pipes. Only write about what you genuinely see on the job.
- **Include photos from jobs in that city** when you have them and the customer is fine with it.
- **List the neighborhoods you cover**, like Westcliff, Eastbluff and Corona del Mar in Newport Beach, or Woodbridge, Turtle Rock and Northwood in Irvine.
- **Link to your relevant service pages** from each city page.

Start with your top two or three cities. A few strong pages beat a dozen weak ones.

## 4. Show Proof That You're the Real Deal

Homeowners are letting a stranger into their house, often while something is going wrong. Trust decides who gets the call.

### Trust signals to add

- **Your California contractor license number**, displayed in the footer or on your About page. Homeowners can look it up, and showing it signals you have nothing to hide.
- **Insurance and bonding information**, if applicable.
- **Real reviews**, either embedded from Google or quoted with the customer's first name and city. Never make up reviews or edit them to say something they didn't.
- **Photos of you, your team and your trucks.** A real face beats a stock photo of a smiling model holding a wrench every time.
- **How long you've been in business** and any certifications or manufacturer training you actually have.
- **Clear pricing approach.** You don't need to post a price list, but explaining how you quote, whether there's a service call fee, and that you give the price before starting work removes a big source of hesitation.

## 5. Make It Fast and Easy on a Phone

Most emergency plumbing searches happen on a phone, often in a stressful moment. If your site takes forever to load or the text is tiny, people bounce back to Google and pick the next result.

### Quick checks you can do today

- Open your site on your phone using cellular data, not Wi-Fi. Does it load quickly?
- Can you read everything without pinching to zoom?
- Are buttons big enough to tap with a thumb?
- Does a pop-up block the screen before someone can see your number?

You can also run your site through Google's free PageSpeed Insights tool to see specific speed problems. Large, uncompressed photos are one of the most common culprits on contractor websites.

## 6. Get the Local SEO Basics Right

These aren't glamorous, but they're the foundation everything else sits on.

### Technical and on-page basics

- **Your name, address and phone number match everywhere.** Your website, Google Business Profile, Yelp, Angi, and other directories should list them exactly the same way.
- **Each page has a unique title tag and meta description** that includes the service and city. For example: "Drain Cleaning in Irvine, CA" rather than "Home" or "Services."
- **Your service area is clear.** If you're a service-area business without a storefront, list the cities you serve on your site and keep that consistent with your Google profile.
- **Use local business schema markup.** This is code that tells search engines your business type, location, hours and contact info. Your web developer can add it, and it helps Google understand your business.
- **Link your website from your Google Business Profile**, and consider pointing the profile link to your most relevant city or service page.
- **Make sure Google can index your pages.** Search "site:yourdomain.com" on Google to see which pages show up.

## 7. Answer the Questions Customers Actually Ask

Every plumber gets the same questions over and over. Answer them on your site and you'll pick up searches your competitors miss.

### Ideas for FAQs and short articles

- How do I know if I have a slab leak?
- Should I repair or replace my water heater?
- Is a tankless water heater worth it for my home?
- What should I do while waiting for an emergency plumber?
- Why does my drain keep clogging?
- Do I need a permit to replace a water heater?

Add a short FAQ section to each service page, and turn the bigger questions into blog posts. Keep answers honest and practical. If the answer is "it depends," explain what it depends on.

## 8. Track Where Your Calls Come From

If you don't know which calls came from your website, you can't tell whether it's working.

### Simple ways to track results

- **Set up Google Analytics and Google Search Console.** Both are free. Search Console shows which searches bring people to your site and which pages rank.
- **Use a call tracking number on your website** that forwards to your main line, so you can see how many calls the site produces. Keep your main number on your Google profile and directories for consistency.
- **Ask every new customer how they found you** and write it down. It's low-tech and it works.

Review the numbers once a month. Over time you'll see which services and cities are pulling their weight and where to focus next.

## Where to Start if Your Site Needs Work

If this list feels long, here's the order I'd go in:

1. Fix the phone number and mobile experience first. That's where calls are being lost right now.
2. Add trust signals like your license number, real reviews and real photos.
3. Build out pages for your two or three most profitable services.
4. Add strong city pages for the areas you most want to work in.
5. Layer in FAQs, blog posts and tracking.

And if your current site is more than a few years old, hard to update, or was never built with local search in mind, it's often faster and cheaper in the long run to rebuild it properly than to patch it piece by piece.

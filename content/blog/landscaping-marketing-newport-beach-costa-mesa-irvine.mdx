Landscaping in Orange County is a visual business sold in a very non-visual way. Most homeowners in Newport Beach, Costa Mesa and Irvine find a landscaper the same way they find a plumber: they pull out their phone, search something like "landscaper near me" or "drought tolerant landscaping Irvine," and look at the first few companies that show up.

If your best work only lives on your phone's camera roll and your truck wrap, you're leaving a lot of jobs on the table. The good news is that landscapers have a natural advantage online. Your work photographs well, your customers love to show off their yards, and most of your competitors still have thin, outdated websites.

This guide walks through how to turn that advantage into steady calls and quote requests.

## Why Landscaping Is Different From Other Trades Online

A few things make landscaping marketing its own thing:

- **People buy with their eyes.** A homeowner planning a backyard redo wants to see what you've built before they ever call. Photos do more selling than anything you write.
- **There are two very different customers.** Someone who wants weekly maintenance is looking for reliability and a fair monthly price. Someone planning a full front-yard redesign is looking for ideas, skill and trust on a much bigger job.
- **Jobs are often planned, not urgent.** Unlike a burst pipe, a new patio or a lawn removal is something people research for weeks. That gives you time to win them over with good content.
- **Local conditions matter.** Coastal yards, inland yards, HOA rules and water use all change what makes sense, and homeowners want someone who understands that.

## 1. Set Up Your Google Business Profile Like a Portfolio

Your Google Business Profile is what gets you into the map results. For landscapers, it's also a free photo gallery that people scroll through before they call.

### What to get right

- **Choose the right primary category.** For most companies that's "Landscaper" or "Landscape designer." Add secondary categories only for services you actually provide, like lawn care service or irrigation.
- **Set your service area** to the cities you really want to work in, such as Newport Beach, Costa Mesa, Irvine, Huntington Beach, Tustin and Laguna Beach.
- **List each service separately**: landscape design, installation, maintenance, irrigation repair, drought tolerant landscaping, sod and turf, hardscape, outdoor lighting, tree and shrub trimming.
- **Upload real project photos every week or two.** Before and after shots are gold. Take them from the same angle so the change is obvious.
- **Ask every happy customer for a review**, and gently suggest they mention the service and their city. A review that says "they redid our front yard in Costa Mesa" helps more than one that just says "great job."
- **Reply to every review** in a friendly, professional voice.

## 2. Give Every Service Its Own Page

Google ranks individual pages. One "Services" page that lists everything in a few bullet points won't rank well for any of it. If you want calls for a specific type of job, give that job its own page.

### Pages most OC landscapers should have

- Landscape design
- Landscape installation
- Weekly or monthly landscape maintenance
- Drought tolerant and low water landscaping
- Lawn removal and turf replacement
- Irrigation installation and repair, including drip systems and smart controllers
- Hardscape, like pavers, patios, walkways and retaining walls, if you do it
- Outdoor and landscape lighting, if you do it

### What each service page should include

- A clear headline with the service and area, like "Drought Tolerant Landscaping in Irvine and Costa Mesa"
- Who the service is for and what problem it solves, in plain English
- Your process, from the first visit and estimate to the finished yard
- A gallery of real photos from that type of job
- A few common questions and honest answers
- A click-to-call button and a short quote request form

## 3. Show Your Work With a Real Project Gallery

This is where landscapers can beat almost every other trade. A strong project gallery sells for you around the clock.

### How to make your gallery work harder

- **Organize projects by type and city.** For example, "Front yard redesign, Newport Heights" or "Backyard pavers and lighting, Irvine." That helps visitors find what they care about and gives Google local context.
- **Write a few sentences for each project.** What the homeowner wanted, what you changed, and what plants or materials you used. Short and specific beats long and vague.
- **Use before and after pairs.** They show the size of the change better than any finished photo alone.
- **Shrink your image files before uploading.** Big photos straight off a phone can slow your site down badly on mobile, which hurts both rankings and calls.
- **Get permission** before posting photos that show a customer's home or address.

## 4. Write Content That Fits Coastal, Inland and HOA Yards

You know the area, so put that knowledge on the page. It's one of the easiest ways to stand out from big, faraway companies.

### Ideas for city pages and local content

- **Newport Beach, Corona del Mar and Laguna Beach.** Coastal yards deal with salt air, wind and sometimes sandy soil. If you've learned which plants hold up near the water and which ones struggle, that's exactly the kind of advice homeowners search for.
- **Irvine.** Many neighborhoods are part of HOAs, and changes to front yards often need approval. If you help customers with plans and plant lists for HOA review, say so clearly. That's a real selling point.
- **Costa Mesa and Tustin.** Lots of older homes with tired lawns and original sprinkler systems, which makes lawn removal, irrigation upgrades and full refreshes common jobs.
- **Huntington Beach.** Similar coastal conditions, plus plenty of homeowners looking for lower maintenance yards.

Only write about what you really see on the job. If a city page could be copied to another city by swapping the name, it isn't helping. Start with two or three of your best cities and make those pages genuinely useful.

## 5. Make Water-Wise Landscaping a Focus

Water use comes up constantly with Southern California homeowners. Plenty of people want to replace thirsty lawns, cut their water bills or simply spend less time on yard work.

### How to own this topic

- **Create a dedicated page** for drought tolerant or low water landscaping, with photos of yards you've converted.
- **Explain your approach** to plant choices, mulch, drip irrigation and smart controllers in plain language.
- **Mention rebates carefully.** Local water agencies have offered turf replacement and irrigation rebates at different times. If you help customers figure out what's currently available, say that, and link to the official program page instead of quoting amounts that may change.
- **Answer the worry about looks.** Many homeowners assume low water means rocks and cactus. Show them yards that are green, colorful and still easy to care for.

## 6. Turn Your Website Into a Quote Request Machine

Ranking gets people to your site. Your website has to turn them into calls and quote requests.

### Quick wins

- **Put a click-to-call phone number at the top of every page**, plus a sticky call button on mobile.
- **Say what you do and where, right at the top.** For example, "Landscape design, installation and maintenance in Newport Beach, Costa Mesa and Irvine."
- **Lead with your best photo**, not a generic stock image of grass.
- **Make the quote form short.** Name, phone, city, type of project and an optional photo upload is plenty.
- **Show trust signals**: your California contractor license number if you hold one, years in business, real reviews and photos of your crew.
- **Be clear about how estimates work.** Tell people whether estimates are free, how soon you can come out and what happens next.
- **Test your site on a phone over cellular data.** Google's free PageSpeed Insights tool will point out what's slowing it down.

## 7. Use the Calendar to Your Advantage

Landscaping has its own rhythm, even in Orange County's mild climate.

- **Fall and winter** are a good time to publish content about planting season, lawn removal and yard planning, since many homeowners start thinking about spring projects early.
- **Spring** is when a lot of people want their yard looking good, so make sure your design and installation pages are strong before then.
- **Summer** brings questions about watering, brown spots and irrigation problems, which is a great time for maintenance and irrigation content.
- **Holidays** can be a nice opening for outdoor lighting, if it's a service you offer.

New and updated pages can take time to rank, so publish a season ahead of when you want the calls.

## 8. Track Where Your Leads Come From

You can't improve what you don't measure.

- **Set up Google Search Console and Google Analytics.** Both are free, and Search Console shows which searches bring people to your site.
- **Check your Google Business Profile performance** to see calls, website clicks and direction requests.
- **Ask every new customer how they found you** and write it down.
- **Note which services and cities bring in the best jobs**, then build more content around those.

## Where to Start

If you're not sure where to begin, here's the order I'd tackle it:

1. Clean up your Google Business Profile and start adding project photos and asking for reviews.
2. Fix your website's phone number, mobile speed and quote form.
3. Build a real project gallery organized by job type and city.
4. Create dedicated pages for your most profitable services, often design and installation or drought tolerant conversions.
5. Add strong city pages for your top two or three service areas.

If your current website is outdated, slow or doesn't show off your work, it's often faster to rebuild it properly than to patch it one piece at a time.
